Yes. I reviewed the selected SIH26227 problem statement and your existing HeisenBug proposal. The important thing is that your prototype should **not look like “a map + satellite images + an AI search bar.”** The problem statement is broader: semantic and image retrieval, multi-temporal change detection, false-alarm suppression, similar-site discovery, analyst review/provenance, incremental ingestion, and fully offline operation. 

Your presentation already frames the flow as **plain-language query → AI understanding → archive search → temporal analysis → verified change report**, with Sentinel-1/2, Landsat, CCDC, geo-object indexing and analyst feedback. 

The prototype I would build around that is:

# HEISENBUG // ORBIT

### **Evidence-first Satellite Intelligence Workstation**

The key visual idea:

> **Don't make the AI answer the analyst. Make the AI build the evidence chain.**

Instead of a conventional dashboard, the entire UI tells one story:

**QUESTION → DISCOVERY → CHANGE TRAJECTORY → EVIDENCE → REVIEW → DECISION**

That is much more memorable in an SIH demo.

# 1. What should make your prototype different

I researched current EO interfaces and satellite-analysis workflows. Existing systems commonly expose maps, layer controls, date comparison, scene lists and swipe/opacity comparison. NASA Worldview, for example, exposes comparison modes such as Swipe, Opacity and Spy and a time scale; Planet exposes scene lists, filters and image comparison. ([Worldview][1])

Copernicus is also increasingly organized around standardized spatio-temporal cataloging and STAC-based search/filtering. ([Stacspec][2])

So your differentiation should **not** be:

> "We have a nicer satellite map."

Instead:

### Your standout layer is the **Evidence Intelligence Layer**

Every detected change should answer:

**What changed?**
**Where?**
**When did it start?**
**Why do we believe it is real?**
**What evidence contradicts it?**
**Which scenes support it?**
**What other locations look similar?**
**What did the analyst decide?**

That aligns exceptionally well with the actual problem statement's requirement for before/after evidence, acquisition time, sensor/source, confidence, processing history and an audit trail.
